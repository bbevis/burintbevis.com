<h1><span class="home-letter-greeting">Hi, I’m Burint!</span>I study how people can disagree constructively</h1>
{{< /home-opening >}}

{{< turn speaker="visitor" >}}
Disagreement doesn’t usually feel very constructive.
{{< /turn >}}

{{< turn >}}
No. But avoiding it isn’t much of a solution either. I’m interested in how we can push back and sharpen each other’s thinking and still leave as friends.
{{< /turn >}}

{{< turn speaker="visitor" >}}
Sounds like a difficult balance. People seem to either avoid the argument altogether or make a mess of it.
{{< /turn >}}

{{< turn >}}
That’s the tension. Keeping quiet can mean leaving something important unsaid, but speaking up can put a relationship under strain. I study what helps people take each other’s ideas seriously without feeling they have to give in.
{{< /turn >}}

{{< turn speaker="visitor" >}}
That sounds good in principle. But how do you actually study something like that?
{{< /turn >}}

{{< turn link="https://github.com/bbevis" linkText="Explore my code on GitHub" >}}
I combine experiments with computational tools to study how people’s words, tone of voice, and facial expressions shape each other’s responses. Experiments let me test what makes a difference, while computational tools help me examine the back-and-forth in detail. Together, they help me understand why some disagreements move us towards a solution while others drive us apart.
{{< /turn >}}

{{< turn speaker="visitor" >}}
How did you get into this? It seems to draw on quite a few different disciplines.
{{< /turn >}}

{{< turn link="/research/" linkText="Explore my research" >}}
I came to this through organizational behavior and data science. I completed my PhD at Imperial College London and my MSc in Data Science at King’s College London. Bringing those perspectives together lets me study a very human question in finer detail.
{{< /turn >}}

{{< turn speaker="visitor" >}}
I imagine your students don’t always agree with you!
{{< /turn >}}

{{< turn link="/teaching/" linkText="Explore my teaching" >}}
I’d be a little concerned if they did. I teach organizational behavior, so these questions come into the classroom too. My research informs what I teach, and sometimes a class discussion challenges my thinking and gives me a new question to investigate.
{{< /turn >}}
